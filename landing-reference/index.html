The limits are in the product, not left to good manners.

  • Teammates can't read your weekly report.

    They read your daily updates. The report goes to managers only, and the server checks that on every request, so typing its URL gets a teammate nothing.

  • Managers comment. They can't edit a word.

    They approve a report, or send it back with a comment. The words stay the author's, in every revision.

  • Nobody reviews their own report.

    Managers review each other too. The server refuses an approval or a send-back on your own week.

Asked before rolling it out.

Is this employee monitoring?

No. Nothing tracks activity, screens or idle time. People write a few lines each working day and one report a week, in their own words. A manager can comment, approve or send a report back. They can't edit a word of it.

What can my teammates see?

Your daily updates, and the comments on them. Your weekly report goes to managers only, and that's checked on the server for every request. Teammates can comment on an update only if you open it to them.

How long does the weekly report take?

The days write most of it. On Friday you give each task a status and a note, pick the key blocker if there is one, and submit. For a normal week that's a few minutes.

What happens when a report is sent back?

It moves to Needs correction and reopens for its author, with the manager's comment attached. The version that was sent back is filed, not replaced.

Do we need Slack, email or another tool?

No. WeeklyView is a standalone web app with no email, no push and no bell. Anything you haven't seen yet carries a small "new" marker until you open it.

How far along is it, and how does a team start?

It's new. Each team runs its own installation: on a fresh one, the first person to sign up becomes the owner and invites everyone else with a link. This copy is the demo, so signup here is closed.
